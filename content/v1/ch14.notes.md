# Vol I, Ch 14 notes (checked against marxists.org ch14.htm, 2026-10-20)

- Verified: period of manufacture from the middle of the 16th to the last third of the 18th
  century; first origin, the carriage made by "wheelwrights, harness-makers, tailors, locksmiths,
  upholsterers, turners, fringe-makers, glaziers, painters, polishers, gilders"; the tailor
  "gradually loses, through want of practice, the ability to carry on ... his old handicraft";
  second origin, paper, type and needles; the Nuremberg needlemaker's "perhaps 20 operations" and
  20 English needlemakers each doing one; heterogeneous (watch, after William Petty) vs serial
  manufacture; needle wire through "72 and sometimes even 92" detail workmen; "a hierarchy of
  labour-powers, to which there corresponds a scale of wages"; skilled and unskilled, "the cost of
  apprenticeship vanishes"; the worker made into a fragment; Adam Smith on simple operations
  dulling the understanding; Ferguson's "nation of Helots"; "anarchy in the social division of
  labour and despotism in that of the workshop"; the "same bourgeois mind" passage.
- Correction to the brief: the brief paired "two origins" with heterogeneous/serial. In Marx these
  are separate ideas (Section 1: two origins, carriage and needle; Section 3: two forms,
  heterogeneous, e.g. the watch, and serial, e.g. the needle). The page keeps them separate.
- Marx does NOT cite Adam Smith's pin factory in this chapter; pins appear only in a Dugald
  Stewart footnote. The source note says so. The critics section mentions Smith's praise of the
  division of labour in general.
- Site choices: five needle stations stand in for 72 to 92; which stations count as "skilled" is
  invented for the picture. Animation 14.3 uses emoji icons for Ava's five skills.
- Critics: Adam Smith on specialisation; lean production / Toyota research. My summaries.
