## Volume I, Chapter 10 (checked against marxists.org, 2026-10-20)

- Verified in the text: "right against right ... Between equal rights force decides"; "were-wolf
  hunger for surplus-labour"; "Capital is dead labour, that, vampire-like, only lives by sucking
  living labour"; "Capital has not invented surplus-labour"; the Règlement organique's 14 corvée
  days that amount to 42; London journeymen bakers start "at about eleven at night", make dough
  (half to three quarters of an hour), sleep about two hours on the kneading board, about five hours
  of rapid work, bakehouse 75 to over 90 degrees Fahrenheit, distribution until between 1 and 6 p.m.;
  bakers "rarely reach the age of 42"; 1863 ban on night work (9 p.m. to 5 a.m.) for bakers under
  18; Mary Anne Walkley, 20, 26½ hours without intermission, June 1863; Statute of Labourers
  (23 Edward III, 1349) and the 1496 statute (5 a.m. to 7 or 8 p.m.); Factory Act 1833 (cotton,
  wool, flax, silk; 13 to 18 at most 12 hours; 9 to 13 at most 8; under 9 forbidden); Act of
  7 June 1844 (women over 18 on the same footing, 12 hours; "for the first time" adults); Act of
  8 June 1847 (10 hours from 1 May 1848 for young persons and women); Act of 1850 (6 a.m. to 6 p.m.
  for young persons and women, 10½ hours Monday to Friday, 60 a week); Leonard Horner's prosecutions;
  Baltimore congress 16 August 1866 and the International's Geneva congress demanding 8 hours;
  "protracted civil war"; "modest Magna Charta of a legally limited working-day"; the greedy farmer
  robbing the soil.
