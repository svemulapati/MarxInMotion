## Volume I, Chapter 9 (checked against marxists.org, 2026-10-20)

- Verified in the text: rate of surplus-value s/v as "an exact expression for the degree of
  exploitation of labour-power by capital"; c set aside because it only reappears; the 20 lb /
  30s yarn split into 16 lb (24s c), 2 lb (3s v), 2 lb (3s s); slave labour vs wage labour differ
  only in how surplus labour is extracted; Senior summoned to Manchester in 1836, pamphlet 1837,
  11½-hour day, 23 half-hours (20 replace capital, 1 machine wear, last 2 net profit); Marx's
  5¾ / 5¾ split; surplus falling to 4¾ hours at 82 14/23%; "all bosh".
- **Check:** Marx's text says the hours fall "from 11½ to 10", but his arithmetic (surplus 5¾ to
  4¾ hours, rate 82 14/23%) matches a one-hour cut, which is also what Senior's claim was about.
  The animation shows a one-hour cut (11½ to 10½). Worth checking against the Fowkes text.
- Critic 2's reply mentions the Ten Hours Act of 1847 and Marx's account in Chapter 10.
