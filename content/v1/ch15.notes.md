## Volume I, Chapter 15 (checked against marxists.org, Moore and Aveling text, 2026-10-20)

- Verified in the text: Mill's remark that it is "questionable if all the mechanical inventions
  yet made have lightened the day's toil of any human being"; motor mechanism, transmitting
  mechanism, tool or working machine; machinery "creates no new value"; "a means for producing
  surplus-value"; moral depreciation; the family of four ("spreads the value of the man's
  labour-power over his whole family"); Ten Hours' Act of 1847 and the speed-up that followed;
  "living appendage"; hand-loom weavers' extinction "sealed in 1838", 2½d. a day, 800,000 with
  families in 1838; the Luddite movement in "the first 15 years of this century"; "It took both
  time and experience ..."; the compensation theory and the carpet-factory example (100 workmen
  at £30, 50 discharged, £1,500 of machinery); education clauses; Owen and "the germ of the
  education of the future"; "the fully developed individual".
- Not used on the page, flag if added later: the "bones of the cotton-weavers are bleaching the
  plains of India" line, which Marx attributes to the Governor General (1834-35). Historians
  dispute that attribution.
- Invented for the animations: Leo's pay (3 coins then 1), cloth price 10 then 2 coins, the
  machine's 1,000-coin value, the 12 to 15 hour stretch in animation 15.2 (Marx gives many real
  cases of long days but not this one), and the speed gauge.
- Children at work: handled gently (Sam's little brother joins the mill). Review tone.
- Critics, my summaries: the long-run rise in real wages and fall in hours; Robert C. Allen's
  high-wage economy explanation of British mechanisation (2009).
