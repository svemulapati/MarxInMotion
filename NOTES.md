# Notes for review

Details that are uncertain or need a human check. Newest first.

## Edition and numbering

- **Volume I chapter titles use the Penguin/Fowkes wording from memory.** The part and chapter
  *numbering* was verified against marxists.org (2026-10-20), but marxists.org hosts the
  1887 Moore and Aveling translation, whose titles differ ("Commodities" vs "The Commodity",
  "Machinery and Modern Industry" vs "Machinery and Large-Scale Industry", and so on). Please
  check the 33 titles in `lib/kapital.ts` against a print copy of the Penguin edition.
- **German editions have 25 chapters in 7 parts** (Fowkes and Moore/Aveling both use 33 in 8).
  The introduction says this; it is from memory and worth a quick check against MEW 23.
- **Volume IV chapter list** follows the Progress Publishers three-part edition on marxists.org
  (24 chapters). Kautsky's original 1905 to 1910 edition is arranged differently. The
  introduction mentions Kautsky as the first editor; the site will follow the Progress edition.

## Volume I, Chapter 1

- Marx's examples used as he gives them: 20 yards of linen = 1 coat; the expanded form with
  10 lb tea, 40 lb coffee, a quarter of corn (shown as "a sack"); 20 yards of linen =
  2 ounces of gold in the money form; English power-looms "probably" halving weaving time
  (the caption says "roughly in half").
- The 10-hour and 5-hour figures are invented for the story and the page says so.
- Critics: Menger and Jevons (marginalism, 1870s) and Böhm-Bawerk's objection about reducing
  skilled to simple labour (*Karl Marx and the Close of His System*, 1896). Both are standard,
  but the summaries are mine.

## Introduction

- Dates used: born 5 May 1818 in Trier; Manifesto 1848; London from 1849; Capital I 1867;
  died 1883; Engels published II in 1885 and III in 1894; Kautsky published Theories of
  Surplus-Value 1905 to 1910.
- "Children as young as eight worked long hours in mills" is a general claim about early
  factory Britain (the 1833 Factory Act set nine as the minimum age in textile mills, which
  implies younger children worked before it). Consider softening or citing.
