# Notes for review

Details that are uncertain or need a human check. Newest first.

## Volume I, Chapters 5 to 18

Written in parallel and merged on 2026-10-20. Every chapter was checked against the
marxists.org text before writing. Items that most need a human eye:

- **Ch 9, Senior's "last hour":** Marx writes the day falls "from 11½ to 10" hours, but his own
  arithmetic (surplus 5¾ to 4¾ hours, rate 82 14/23%) only fits a one-hour cut. The animation
  follows the arithmetic (11½ to 10½).
- **Ch 11.3:** "8 times" read as 8 workers (2 give the master a worker's living, 4 twice that,
  8 if half the surplus is also reinvested). Checked against the text; reading confirmed.
- **Ch 14:** Marx does not cite Smith's pin factory in this chapter (pins only appear in a
  footnote quoting Dugald Stewart), so the page does not claim it. Animation 14.3 uses emoji
  icons for Ava's skills, unlike the drawn style elsewhere.
- **Ch 15:** the "bones of the cotton-weavers" line was left out because its attribution is
  disputed. Child labour (15.2) is shown gently: Sam's mum, sister and brother join the mill.
- **Ch 16:** Fowkes says "subsumption", Moore and Aveling "subjection"; both are mentioned.
- **Ch 17:** older English editions misprint "6 sh." in section 1; the corrected 3 shillings is used.
- **Fixed at merge:** Ch 12 no longer says Tiny Town passed a twelve-hour law (Ch 15 has the day
  stretched to 15 hours by machinery, as Marx describes). Ch 18 now gives rent to a landlord,
  not Grandpa Ned, who rents his own land.
- **Invented numbers** (each labelled on the page): Ch 15 Leo's pay and the 1,000-coin machine;
  Ch 16's 10/5/5 teacher split; Ch 17.2 and 18.1 last steps.
- **Critics** in every chapter are summarised in our own words and deserve a fairness read.

### Volume I, Chapter 18

- Verified in the text: formula I (s/v = surplus-value/value of labour-power = surplus-labour/
  necessary labour); the classical "derivative formulae" II (surplus-labour/working day,
  surplus-value/value of the product, surplus-product/total product) "falsely" express the degree
  of exploitation; with a 12-hour day split 6 and 6 and 3 shillings each, 100% versus 50%; under
  formula II surplus "can never reach 100%"; Lavergne's estimate that the English agricultural
  labourer gets 1/4, so 300%; the "false semblance of an association"; formula III, unpaid
  labour/paid labour, as "only a popular expression"; the capitalist pays the value of
  labour-power; "Capital ... is essentially the command over unpaid labour"; profit, interest and
  rent as forms of unpaid labour.
- Moore and Aveling title: "Various Formula for the Rate of Surplus-Value". Fowkes title as in
  lib/kapital.ts ("Different Formulae ...") is from memory; please check.
- Animation 18.1's last step (1 hour necessary, 1100% versus 92%) extends Marx's argument with
  invented numbers to show the ceiling; Marx himself argues it in words.
- Bea's loan conversation is this site's story.
- Critics are my summaries: Samuelson 1971 on labour values as an unnecessary detour; the
  mainstream view that no hours are unpaid.

### Volume I, Chapter 17

- Verified in the text: the three factors (length, intensity, productivity); the three laws when
  only productivity varies; "Surplus-value and the value of labour-power vary in opposite
  directions"; 6 shillings split 3 + 3, becoming 2 + 4 (4 hours instead of 6) or 4 + 2 (8 hours);
  workers may get more necessaries while the value of labour-power falls, "the abyss ... keep
  widening"; the outcome depends on "the pressure of capital" and "the resistance of the labourer";
  intensified 12 hours creating 7, 8 or more shillings with both parts rising from 3 to 4;
  lengthening by 2 hours at the same price raises surplus-value; case IV A with necessary labour
  6 to 8 hours and the day 12 to 14 (surplus 3s) and 16 (surplus 4s); England 1799 to 1815, money
  wages up, real wages down; case IV B, rising productivity makes a shorter day possible; "spare
  time is acquired for one class by converting the whole life-time of the masses into labour time".
- marxists.org notes that earlier English translations misprint "6 sh." for 3 shillings in
  Section 1; the corrected figure (3) is used here.
- Lavergne's 300% example belongs to Chapter 18, not 17.
- Mia's new oven and the bad harvest are illustrations, not Marx's examples.
- Critics are my summaries: long-run real wage growth and shorter weeks; Kaldor's 1961 "stylised
  facts" on stable shares, and the falling labour share since about 1980.
- Animation 17.2, last step: "productivity up, necessary labour 3 hours, an 8-hour day" is an
  illustration of case IV B, not a figure Marx gives.

### Volume I, Chapter 16

### Volume I, Chapter 15

- Verified in the text: Mill's remark that it is "questionable if all the mechanical inventions
  yet made have lightened the day's toil of any human being"; motor mechanism, transmitting
  mechanism, tool or working machine; machinery "creates no new value"; "a means for producing
  surplus-value"; moral depreciation; the family of four ("spreads the value of the man's
  labour-power over his whole family"); Ten Hours' Act of 1847 and the speed-up that followed;
  "living appendage"; hand-loom weavers' extinction "sealed in 1838", 2½d. a day, 800,000 with
  families in 1838; the Luddite movement in "the first 15 years of this century"; "It took both
  time and experience ..."; the compensation theory and the carpet-factory example (100 workmen
  at £30, 50 discharged, £1,500 of machinery); education clauses; Owen and "the germ of the
  education of the future"; "the fully developed individual".
- Not used on the page, flag if added later: the "bones of the cotton-weavers are bleaching the

- Invented for the animations: Leo's pay (3 coins then 1), cloth price 10 then 2 coins, the
  machine's 1,000-coin value, the 12 to 15 hour stretch in animation 15.2 (Marx gives many real
  cases of long days but not this one), and the speed gauge.
- Children at work: handled gently (Sam's little brother joins the mill). Review tone.
- Critics, my summaries: the long-run rise in real wages and fall in hours; Robert C. Allen's
  high-wage economy explanation of British mechanisation (2009).

### Volume I, Chapter 14

- Verified: period of manufacture from the middle of the 16th to the last third of the 18th
  century; first origin, the carriage made by "wheelwrights, harness-makers, tailors, locksmiths,
  upholsterers, turners, fringe-makers, glaziers, painters, polishers, gilders"; the tailor
  "gradually loses, through want of practice, the ability to carry on ... his old handicraft";
  second origin, paper, type and needles; the Nuremberg needlemaker's "perhaps 20 operations" and
  20 English needlemakers each doing one; heterogeneous (watch, after William Petty) vs serial
  manufacture; needle wire through "72 and sometimes even 92" detail workmen; "a hierarchy of
  labour-powers, to which there corresponds a scale of wages"; skilled and unskilled, "the cost of
  apprenticeship vanishes"; the worker made into a fragment; Adam Smith on simple operations
  dulling the understanding; Ferguson's "nation of Helots"; "anarchy in the social division of
  labour and despotism in that of the workshop"; the "same bourgeois mind" passage.
- Correction to the brief: the brief paired "two origins" with heterogeneous/serial. In Marx these
  are separate ideas (Section 1: two origins, carriage and needle; Section 3: two forms,
  heterogeneous, e.g. the watch, and serial, e.g. the needle). The page keeps them separate.
- Marx does NOT cite Adam Smith's pin factory in this chapter; pins appear only in a Dugald
  Stewart footnote. The source note says so. The critics section mentions Smith's praise of the
  division of labour in general.
- Site choices: five needle stations stand in for 72 to 92; which stations count as "skilled" is
  invented for the picture. Animation 14.3 uses emoji icons for Ava's five skills.
- Critics: Adam Smith on specialisation; lean production / Toyota research. My summaries.

### Volume I, Chapter 13

  stands in for Marx's "raising a heavy weight".
- Critics: Coase (1937), Alchian and Demsetz (1972). My summaries.

### Volume I, Chapter 12

- Verified: definitions of absolute and relative surplus-value; Marx's own illustration of 10
  necessary hours falling to 9 inside a 12-hour day; only productivity in industries making
  necessities (or their means of production) lowers the value of labour-power ("leaves the value
  of labour-power undisturbed" otherwise); shirts as one necessity among many; the 12 articles at
  1s (6d means of production + 6d new value), doubled to 24 at 9d, sold at 10d for an extra 1d;
  "this extra surplus-value vanishes, so soon as the new method of production has become general";
  "coercive laws of competition".
- Site choices: animations 12.1 and 12.2 use the workshop numbers (6 hours, 3 coins) falling to
  4 hours / 2 coins; the source note says Marx's own example was 10 to 9. Bread and clothes each
  halving (1 coin to 1/2) is invented. The "rival workshop" is a gray one-off figure.
- The story mentions "a law: twelve hours". That relies on Ch 10 (Factory Acts) in the story;
  check it matches whatever Ch 10 says Tiny Town's law is.
- Critics: rising real wages (general), Schumpeter on temporary innovator profits. My summaries.

### Volume I, Chapter 11

- Verified in the text: mass of surplus-value = variable capital advanced × rate of surplus-value;
  the 500 labourers / 1,500s. against 100 labourers at 200% or an 18-hour day / 600s., never
  reaching 1,200s. (24 hours); "contradicts all experience based on appearance" with the cotton
  spinner and the baker; the minimum sum: a worker needing 8 hours, 4 hours of surplus labour,
  two labourers to live like a labourer, "8 times" the minimum capital to live twice as well and
  turn half the surplus into capital; the "small master"; medieval guilds limiting labourers per
  master; Hegel's quantity into quality; "capital acquired the command over labour"; "a coercive
  relation"; "no longer the labourer that employs the means of production, but the means of
  production that employ the labourer".
- Animation 11.3 reads Marx's "8 times" as 8 helpers (8 × the cost of one labour-power): 2 helpers
  give 8 surplus hours (one living); twice as well needs 16; saving half doubles it to 32 hours,
  which is 8 helpers at 4 hours each. Please double-check this reading.
- Animation 11.1 uses the CONTINUITY.md workshop numbers (3 coins of wage, 3 of surplus per worker);
  11.2 uses Marx's shilling numbers relabelled as coins.
- Critics are my summaries: low-headcount, high-profit firms with Böhm-Bawerk's objection to the
  Volume III solution; the persistence of small businesses.

### Volume I, Chapter 10

- Verified in the text: "right against right ... Between equal rights force decides"; "were-wolf
  hunger for surplus-labour"; "Capital is dead labour, that, vampire-like, only lives by sucking
  living labour"; "Capital has not invented surplus-labour"; the Règlement organique's 14 corvée
  days that amount to 42; London journeymen bakers start "at about eleven at night", make dough
  (half to three quarters of an hour), sleep about two hours on the kneading board, about five hours
  of rapid work, bakehouse 75 to over 90 degrees Fahrenheit, distribution until between 1 and 6 p.m.;
  bakers "rarely reach the age of 42"; 1863 ban on night work (9 p.m. to 5 a.m.) for bakers under
  18; Mary Anne Walkley, 20, 26½ hours without intermission, June 1863; Statute of Labourers
  (23 Edward III, 1349) and the 1496 statute (5 a.m. to 7 or 8 p.m.); Factory Act 1833 (cotton,
  wool, flax, silk; 13 to 18 at most 12 hours; 9 to 13 at most 8; under 9 forbidden); Act of
  7 June 1844 (women over 18 on the same footing, 12 hours; "for the first time" adults); Act of
  8 June 1847 (10 hours from 1 May 1848 for young persons and women); Act of 1850 (6 a.m. to 6 p.m.
  for young persons and women, 10½ hours Monday to Friday, 60 a week); Leonard Horner's prosecutions;
  Baltimore congress 16 August 1866 and the International's Geneva congress demanding 8 hours;
  "protracted civil war"; "modest Magna Charta of a legally limited working-day"; the greedy farmer
  robbing the soil.
- Animation 10.2 converts 75 to 90+ °F into "could be hotter than 30 degrees Celsius".
- Mia having once been a baker's helper in the big city is this site's invention, to fit
  CONTINUITY.md ("Mia's bakery is where long hours and night work show up").
- "Inspector Horner" is drawn as a gray one-off figure.
- Critics are my summaries: R. M. Hartwell (optimist side of the standard-of-living debate) and
  Clark Nardinelli, *Child Labor and the Industrial Revolution* (1990). Worth a read for fairness.

### Volume I, Chapter 9

- Verified in the text: rate of surplus-value s/v as "an exact expression for the degree of
  exploitation of labour-power by capital"; c set aside because it only reappears; the 20 lb /

  The animation shows a one-hour cut (11½ to 10½). Worth checking against the Fowkes text.
- Critic 2's reply mentions the Ten Hours Act of 1847 and Marx's account in Chapter 10.

### Volume I, Chapter 7

- Verified in the text: the three elementary factors of the labour process (work, its subject,
  its instruments); the bee and "the worst architect"; the two features of the labour process under
  capital (work under the capitalist's control; the product is his property); 10 lb of cotton for
  10s, 2s of spindle wear, 3s for a day's labour-power, a 6-hour day giving 15s yarn for 15s
  advanced ("Our capitalist stares in astonishment"); the excuses (abstinence, "out of nothing",
  his own superintendence) ending with the capitalist's "hearty laugh"; the 12-hour day where 27s
  become 30s; "The trick has at last succeeded".
- Title: Fowkes "The Labour Process and the Valorization Process"; Moore/Aveling "The
  Labour-Process and the Process of Producing Surplus-Value". The glossary entry for valorization
  mentions the Moore/Aveling wording.
- Critics are my summaries: J. B. Clark's marginal productivity; Nozick's voluntary-exchange
  objection (Anarchy, State, and Utopia, ch. 8). Worth a fairness read.

### Volume I, Chapter 6

- Verified in the text: labour-power as "the aggregate of those mental and physical capabilities";
  sold "only for a definite period", otherwise he would turn "from a free man into a slave"; the
  "free labourer, free in the double sense"; "Nature does not produce on the one side owners of
  money ... and on the other men possessing nothing but their own labour-power" (a historical
  result); children ("his substitutes") and education or training enter the value; "a historical
  and moral element"; the labourer "everywhere gives credit to the capitalist"; "a very Eden of the
  innate rights of man ... Freedom, Equality, Property and Bentham"; "the hidden abode of
  production" and the sign "No admittance except on business".
- Site choices: the 3-coin day and its split (food 1.2, roof 0.6, clothes and firewood 0.4,
  children 0.5, normal comforts 0.3) are invented to illustrate; only the 3-coin total comes from
  CONTINUITY.md (Marx's 3 shillings). Marx's closing image of the worker bringing "his own hide to
  market" with nothing to expect but "a hiding" was softened to "Sam follows slowly, holding back"
  for young readers.
- Bentham is glossed as "a thinker who said people follow their own interest". That is the sense
  Marx uses him in; Bentham's utilitarianism is broader (the greatest happiness of the greatest
  number). Consider rewording.
- Critics (my summaries): marginal-product wage theory; Robert Nozick on free contracts.

## Volume I, Chapters 2 to 4 (checked against marxists.org, 2026-10-20)

## Introduction

- Dates used: born 5 May 1818 in Trier; Manifesto 1848; London from 1849; Capital I 1867;
  died 1883; Engels published II in 1885 and III in 1894; Kautsky published Theories of
  Surplus-Value 1905 to 1910.
- "Children as young as eight worked long hours in mills" is a general claim about early
  factory Britain (the 1833 Factory Act set nine as the minimum age in textile mills, which
  implies younger children worked before it). Consider softening or citing.
